# Protocols

The app ↔ CLI websocket is [the local websocket](local-ws.md).

## CLI ↔ relay

Templates for a bot on another Runner use sealed `templates.memories` and `templates.export.preview` requests, so that Runner redacts its own plugin secrets; an import for another Runner is a `templates.import` request with the file's text and the recipient's choices, never a path, and that Runner checks them again and publishes the new bot through the encrypted roster.

Shared project context uses `projects.get` / `projects.save` / `projects.refresh` / `projects.asset` / `projects.asset_path` on the local API and `projects.changed` events. [Shared project context](project-context.md#local-api) defines their fields. The Mac and the desktop app call them for a group's Project section. Independent encrypted `project_context` blobs carry `{ chat_id, entry }`, one immutable revision per slot, grouped by the existing chat id. First sync pulls these revisions before transcript pages, so discovery includes briefs older than the initial message page.

Protocol **3** accepts the opaque `task`, `handoff`, `review`, `attention`, `project_context`, `playbook`, and `event` blob kinds alongside the existing kinds. Task records use account-DEK encryption and independent latest-only slots, outside roster snapshots and chat groups. Task sync and first sync include that kind; replicas advance by revision. `event` joins the sealed-to-machine kinds and their consume/retention lifecycle. The subject modules own the meanings and authority of the other encrypted kinds.

- Every request, the sync socket's upgrade included, carries `Lorca-Protocol: <n>`, the relay protocol the build speaks (`relay::PROTOCOL` in the CLI; the relay's own is in `/v1/health`), and a `User-Agent` of `lorca/<version> (<os>)`. The relay's client is its own `reqwest` client, so neither reaches a provider. The number goes up when Devices come to rely on something new from the relay or the relay stops serving something old; 1 is group paging and `DELETE /v1/identity`, 2 is `POST /v1/machines`, which a Device without the identity key pairs with, 4 is the [signed envelopes](relay-trust.md#signed-envelopes) and the [account key's generations](relay-trust.md#the-account-key), which an older Device cannot read. A relay started with `--min-protocol` answers a lower number (none is 0) with `426` on every `/v1` route but the healthcheck. The CLI then sets `relay_update_required` (in `hello`, the snapshot, and `relay.status`), waits fifteen minutes between tries instead of backing off from two seconds, and clears it when a session connects or the relay URL changes. The desktop app says so once per launch in a sheet with Check for Updates, and its Device pane reads "update Lorca to sync"; the phone shows "Update Lorca to sync" in the chat list's title slot and in Settings.
- Machine bearer for blobs, the sync socket, the machine list, and a paired machine attesting another; identity signature for registering the identity and for the machines an identity device attests.
- PUT/GET non-file blobs as base64url ciphertext in JSON, and PUT/GET files as binary ciphertext. `roster` is a whole-roster snapshot of bots, chats, sidebar sections, and routines (latest wins), with `deleted`, the ids of the bots, chats, and routines deleted on any Device. A Device applying one keeps every deleted id for good (`State::deleted`), drops what they name, and keeps a bot, chat, or routine the roster leaves out that no Device deleted (`sync::merge_roster_entries`), then uploads the merged roster again: a Device that had not pulled yet, such as a phone back from the background, writes a roster without what another Device just made, and that roster deletes nothing; `chat` is one upsert or removal of a message; `machine` is a Device’s metadata, the turns in flight on it, its [limits](budgets.md#storage-protocol-and-resuming) and what its work used, and the chat its app shows in front of the user ([Sidebar sections and alerts](sidebar.md#the-chat-in-front-on-a-computer)); `credentials` is the account's provider credentials; `review` is one Runner-owned durable review record; `key` is the [account keyring](relay-trust.md#the-account-key).
- Handoffs: `kind=handoff` encrypted with the account DEK, with independent request, Runner-report and requester-cancellation slots per attempt. Each update includes the request, so results can merge before the request’s own blob. `Job.handoff` binds a turn to the contract or to the requesting bot’s result continuation ([Handoffs](handoffs.md)).
- Jobs: `kind=job` with `recipient_machine_pubkey`, a [signed envelope](relay-trust.md#signed-envelopes) sealed to that machine’s box key, deleted by the Runner after the turn.
- Events: `kind=event` with `recipient_machine_pubkey` and no slot or group, a gateway's signed envelope sealed to the Runner's box key. The Runner stores it in its encrypted inbox before its sync cursor moves past it, then deletes the blob ([Event triggers](event-triggers.md#relay-transport)).
- Questions: `kind=request` signed and sealed to one Runner (`crates/cli/src/requests.rs`: `{ id, verb, body }`, asked by its signer), answered with a `kind=response` the Runner signs and seals to the Device that asked (`{ request_id, body, error? }`); each side deletes the blob it consumed, and the asker gives up after 20 s, or 75 s for the start of a plugin sign-in, whose discovery and registration the Runner cuts off at 45 s, or 150 s for `mcp.reconnect`, which answers once a server has connected or failed. The `feedback.*` verbs read, record, review and decide workflow revisions on the assigned Runner through the same sealed request/response path. The other verbs are `memory.read` and `memory.write`, so a bot's memory is shown and edited from any Device; `budgets.*` and `connector_limits.*`, so a bot's limits and call limits are set from any Device; `plugins.install` / `plugins.rename` / `plugins.uninstall` / `plugins.variables` / `plugins.connect` / `plugins.sign_out` / `plugins.detail`, so plugins on a Runner are managed from any Device, and `plugins.sign_in.finish` / `plugins.sign_in.cancel`, with which the Device that asked for a sign-in (its `redirect_uri` on `plugins.connect` or `permission.answer`) hands back where its browser landed (see Sign-in in [Codemode and Plugins](plugins.md#plugins)); the `mcp.*` methods but `mcp.parse`, so a Runner's own MCP servers are managed from any Device; `permissions.catalog`, so a bot's Access sheet lists its Runner's plugins and their tools; `permission.answer`, so a permission card is answered from any Device and a secret request's values reach its Runner as ciphertext; `bash.stdin`, `bash.stop`, and `bash.background`, so a command a bot runs is answered, stopped, or sent to the background from any Device; `coding.transcript`, `coding.stop`, `coding.set`, and `coding.answer`, so a coding agent's transcript is read, and the agent stopped, set, or answered, from any Device; `secrets.list`, `secrets.set`, and `secrets.delete`, so a Runner's secrets are listed without their values, replaced, or deleted from any Device; `chats.send_now`, so a message the bot's turn holds is read at once from any Device; the `events.*` methods but `events.forward`, so a Runner's event subscriptions are managed from any Device; `service.status`, so the Devices pane shows whether `lorca service` runs on a Runner; and `update.install` and `update.auto`, so a self-updating Runner's CLI installs its latest release, or turns its automatic updates on or off, from any Device. A verb runs here when this Device is the Runner, else it is asked (`requests::on_runner`), and refused up front when the Runner is unknown or offline.
- Slots: a `review` (hashed `review/<id>` slot), `roster`, `chat`, `machine`, or `credentials` blob goes up with a `slot` (`Slot` in `app.rs`, `ChatBlob::slot`), and the relay deletes the blobs of that slot it supersedes, so the log holds a message once and not once per streamed chunk. A message's versions and its removal share the slot named by the message id. An upsert sets `keep_first`, which spares the slot's oldest blob: its seq holds the message's place in the log, so a Device that replays from the start gets the transcript in order, first the early version and later the final one. A tool row is the exception: its running version goes up without arguments or results, for the status line on other Devices, and the finished row supersedes it outright, so the relay keeps one version of each call, at the finished row's place. A row that shows a terminal keeps its first place as a message does, since the transcript shows it. A removal supersedes every version. The SQLite outbox does the same before upload: a queued version updates its row without moving its queue position. `App::upsert_message` holds one lock from the SQLite write that gives a message its `position` to the enqueue, so the sending Device's transcript order is the log's order.
- Groups: a `chat` blob and a message's `file` blob go up with `group`, the chat's id (`ChatBlob::group`). Deleting a chat queues its id in `State::group_deletes`, drops what the outbox still held for it, and the sync cycle calls `DELETE /v1/groups/{group}` until it lands: the relay deletes the group's rows and the attachments' objects in one transaction, gives the bytes back to the quota, and remembers the group in `deleted_groups`, so a blob that names it later (a Runner finishing a turn in a chat another Device deleted) gets `409` and the outbox drops it. The other Devices learn of the deletion from the roster. A bot's avatar is a `file` with no group: the Device that replaces or removes it, or deletes its bot, removes its local copy and queues the attachment id in `State::blob_deletes`, and the sync cycle calls `DELETE /v1/blobs/{id}` until it lands, which removes the row and the object.
- `GET /v1/groups/{group}/blobs?before=<place>&limit=` reads a chat backwards a message at a time: `{ slots: [{ place, blobs }], has_more }`, oldest first, where a slot is a message (its first and latest blob, or its removal) or the chat's read mark, and `place` is the slot's lowest seq. A message other than a tool row keeps the place its first version took however late its last one landed, so pages read backwards give the transcript in the log's order. A page is up to 500 slots and 8 MiB, and leaves out the group's `file` blobs. A relay without the route answers `404`, and the Device replays the log.
- `GET /v1/blobs?since=` answers a page of up to 8 MiB of ciphertext (and at least one blob); a Device asks again from the last seq it got. A Device at seq 0 first takes `roster,machine` alone, so chats have their names and bots before their messages land.
- Every Device keeps `last_seq` and its outbox in `lorca.sqlite3`; queued ciphertext is a binary SQLite BLOB. Uploads retry until the relay accepts them. Attachment transfers have a ten-minute request timeout.

```
App_B → CLI_B
CLI_B encrypts user message + job-for-A → relay
CLI_A fetches envelopes for A, decrypts, runs loop, encrypts reply
CLI_B fetches chat blobs, decrypts → App_B
```
